Lecture 5

The Basic English Structure

Introduction

Technical writing depends on correct grammar, sentence structure, and word usage. This lecture covers the essential building blocks of English — parts of speech, sentence patterns, common errors, and numerical structures.

Part 1: Parts of Speech

Part of SpeechDefinitionExample
NounNames a person, place, animal, idea, or thingstudent, research, capsule
PronounSubstitutes a nounher, she, it
VerbDenotes an action by the subjectwrote, pitched

Part 2: Sentence Structure Components

  • Subject — performs the action. "The child kept crying."
  • Verb — shows the action. "The child kept crying."
  • Object — a noun/pronoun that receives the action. "My mom made a cake."
  • Complement — words required to complete the meaning. "The students are very excited."
  • Adjunct — gives more information. "Yesterday, we met at the park."

Part 4: Common Sentence Errors

Sentence Fragments

A word, phrase, or clause that lacks a subject, a verb, or a complete thought.

Examples

❌ "As soon as I saw her."
❌ "Because she forgot her umbrella."
✅ "I waved as soon as I saw her."

Run-on Sentences

Occur when independent clauses are improperly joined.

Examples

❌ "She wanted to analyze the errors but she didn't have samples."
✅ "She wanted to analyze the errors, but she didn't have samples."

Part 5: Parallelism

The use of matching sentence structure, phrases, or longer parts so as to balance ideas of equal importance.

Rule 1: Lists must use the same syntactic form

✅ "I had grilled steak, mashed potatoes, and stir-fried vegetables for breakfast."

Rule 2: Listed points must have the same structure

The types of prepositions are: Prepositions of Time, of Place, of Location, of Direction, of Spatial Relationship.

Rule 3: Actions must use the same verb forms

✅ "Setting a timetable, studying daily lessons, and keeping track of progress will help you achieve good grades."

Part 6: Misplaced Modifiers

A modifier placed too far from the word it modifies.

❌ "The patient was referred to the physician with stomach pains."
✅ "The patient with stomach pains was referred to the physician."

Part 7: Subject-Verb Agreement

  • Compound subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb. "Caren, Sheela, and Akash have completed their assessments."
  • Subjects joined by "or" take a singular verb. "Celery or spring onion works fine."
  • Indefinite pronouns are singular. "Everyone is happy with the outcome."
  • With "either/or" and "neither/nor," the verb agrees with the nearer subject. "Neither the children nor their parent is aware."
  • Abstract nouns are singular. "Honesty is the best policy."
  • Periods of time, distances, and sums of money take singular verbs. "10 years is not considered optimum."
  • Subjunctive mood uses "were" for wishes. "I wish I were a bird."

Part 8: Numerical Structures in Writing

Money

  • Use numerals and currency symbols: $1,250.50, PHP 5,000, €350
  • Avoid unnecessary decimals: $100 (not $100.00)
  • Spell out amounts at the start of a sentence: "Five hundred dollars was allocated."

Dates

  • MM/DD/YYYY (American): 02/25/2025
  • DD/MM/YYYY (British): 25/02/2025
  • Month Day, Year (Formal): February 25, 2025
  • Technical (ISO): 2025-02-25

Time

  • 12-hour: 9:30 AM, 6:45 PM
  • 24-hour: 09:30, 18:45
  • Ranges: 10:00 AM – 2:00 PM or 14:00 to 16:30

Numbers, Decimals, and Percentages

  • Spell out numbers below 10; use numerals for 10 and above
  • Use numerals for measurements and statistics: 5 cm, 2.5 kg
  • Use decimals for precision: 2.75 cm
  • Use numerals + % for percentages: 25%
  • Always use a leading zero: 0.5 (not .5)

Units of Measurement

  • Follow SI units: km, m, g, L
  • Be consistent with spacing: 25 kg (not 25kg)

Self-Check

1. What is a sentence fragment?

A word, phrase, or clause that lacks a subject, verb, or complete thought.

2. What is a comma splice?

Two independent clauses joined by a comma without a coordinating conjunction.

3. Fix for parallelism: "She likes reading, writing, and to draw."

"She likes reading, writing, and drawing."
